Global Positioning System

The Global Positioning System, or GPS, is a satellite-based navigation system that provides location and time information. It was developed by the United States Department of Defense and later became widely available for civilian use.

GPS works by measuring signals from multiple satellites. A receiver estimates its distance from each satellite based on signal travel time. With signals from at least four satellites, the receiver can estimate latitude, longitude, altitude, and clock correction.

GPS is used in navigation, mapping, aviation, shipping, agriculture, emergency response, construction, telecommunications, and scientific measurement. Smartphones, vehicles, drones, and wearable devices commonly use GPS along with other sensors.

GPS accuracy can be affected by buildings, mountains, atmospheric conditions, signal blockage, satellite geometry, and receiver quality. Other satellite navigation systems include Galileo, GLONASS, BeiDou, and regional augmentation systems.
