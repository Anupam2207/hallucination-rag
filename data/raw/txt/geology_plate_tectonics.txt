Plate Tectonics

Plate tectonics is the scientific theory that Earth's outer shell is divided into large moving plates. These plates move slowly over the more ductile asthenosphere. Their interactions shape many geological features, including mountains, ocean basins, earthquakes, volcanoes, and trenches. Plate boundaries can be divergent, convergent, or transform.

At divergent boundaries, plates move apart and new crust can form, as at mid-ocean ridges. At convergent boundaries, plates move toward each other, producing subduction zones, mountain ranges, or volcanic arcs. At transform boundaries, plates slide past each other, causing earthquakes. Plate motion is driven by forces related to mantle convection, slab pull, and ridge push. Plate tectonics explains the distribution of fossils, rock types, and continents over geological time.
