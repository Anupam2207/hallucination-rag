Apollo 11 Moon Landing

Apollo 11 was the first mission to land humans on the Moon. NASA launched Apollo 11 from Kennedy Space Center on July 16, 1969. The crew members were Neil Armstrong, Buzz Aldrin, and Michael Collins.

The lunar module Eagle landed in the Sea of Tranquility on July 20, 1969. Neil Armstrong became the first person to walk on the Moon. Buzz Aldrin joined him on the lunar surface, while Michael Collins remained in lunar orbit aboard the command module Columbia.

The astronauts collected lunar samples, deployed scientific instruments, took photographs, and demonstrated human exploration beyond Earth. The mission returned safely to Earth on July 24, 1969.

Apollo 11 is historically important because it fulfilled the United States goal of landing humans on the Moon and returning them safely before the end of the 1960s. It also advanced engineering, navigation, communication, and spaceflight operations.
