The Rock Cycle

The rock cycle describes how rocks change among igneous, sedimentary, and metamorphic forms over geological time. Igneous rocks form when molten rock cools and solidifies. Sedimentary rocks form when sediments are deposited, compacted, and cemented. Metamorphic rocks form when existing rocks are changed by heat, pressure, or chemically active fluids without fully melting.

Weathering breaks rocks into smaller particles, and erosion transports those particles. Burial can turn sediments into sedimentary rock. Heat and pressure can transform sedimentary or igneous rocks into metamorphic rock. Melting produces magma, which can cool into igneous rock. The rock cycle is not a simple circle with a fixed order; rocks can follow many pathways depending on geological conditions.
