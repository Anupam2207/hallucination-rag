Crop Rotation

Crop rotation is the practice of growing different crops on the same field in a planned sequence across seasons or years. Farmers use crop rotation to maintain soil fertility, reduce pest pressure, and improve long-term yields. For example, rotating cereals with legumes can improve nitrogen availability because legumes host bacteria that fix atmospheric nitrogen in root nodules.

Crop rotation can also interrupt the life cycles of insects, weeds, and plant diseases that specialize in one crop. A field planted with the same crop every year may develop nutrient depletion and more severe pest problems. Rotations often include grains, legumes, oilseeds, cover crops, and fallow periods depending on climate and market needs. Crop rotation is a core practice in sustainable agriculture because it reduces dependence on chemical inputs and supports healthier soil structure.
