The Carbon Cycle

The carbon cycle describes how carbon moves among the atmosphere, oceans, land, living organisms, and rocks. Plants remove carbon dioxide from the atmosphere through photosynthesis and store carbon in biomass. Animals and microbes return carbon dioxide through respiration and decomposition. Oceans absorb and release carbon dioxide, and they store large amounts of carbon in dissolved forms.

Over long timescales, carbon is stored in rocks and fossil fuels. Human activities such as burning coal, oil, and natural gas transfer carbon that was stored underground into the atmosphere as carbon dioxide. Deforestation can also reduce carbon storage in ecosystems. The carbon cycle is central to climate science because carbon dioxide is a greenhouse gas that affects Earth's energy balance.
