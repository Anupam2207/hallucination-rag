Earth Atmosphere

Earth's atmosphere is the layer of gases surrounding the planet. It is mostly nitrogen and oxygen, with smaller amounts of argon, carbon dioxide, water vapor, and trace gases. The atmosphere protects life by absorbing harmful solar radiation, moderating temperature, and supporting the water cycle.

The main atmospheric layers are the troposphere, stratosphere, mesosphere, thermosphere, and exosphere. Weather occurs mostly in the troposphere, the lowest layer. The stratosphere contains the ozone layer, which absorbs much ultraviolet radiation from the Sun.

Air pressure decreases with altitude because there is less air above a higher location. Temperature patterns vary by layer. In the troposphere, temperature generally decreases with altitude, while in the stratosphere temperature can increase because ozone absorbs ultraviolet radiation.

The atmosphere is central to climate and weather. Greenhouse gases such as carbon dioxide, methane, and water vapor absorb infrared radiation and help keep Earth warmer than it would be without an atmosphere. Human activities can change atmospheric composition and influence climate.
