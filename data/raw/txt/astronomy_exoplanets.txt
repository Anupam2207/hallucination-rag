Exoplanets

An exoplanet is a planet that orbits a star outside the Solar System. Thousands of exoplanets have been confirmed using methods such as the transit method and the radial velocity method. The transit method detects a small dimming of starlight when a planet passes in front of its host star. The radial velocity method measures tiny shifts in a star's spectrum caused by the gravitational pull of an orbiting planet.

Exoplanets vary widely in size, temperature, and composition. Some are gas giants larger than Jupiter, while others are rocky worlds similar in size to Earth. A planet in the habitable zone has an orbit where liquid water could exist on its surface if atmospheric conditions are suitable. The habitable zone does not guarantee life; it only indicates a possible temperature range. Space telescopes such as Kepler and TESS have expanded the catalog of known exoplanets and helped scientists study planetary systems beyond our own.
