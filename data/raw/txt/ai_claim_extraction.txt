Atomic Claim Extraction

Atomic claim extraction splits an answer into small factual units. A sentence such as "RAG retrieves documents and uses them to ground generation" contains two claims: RAG retrieves documents, and RAG uses those documents to ground generation.

A fast extractor can begin with sentence splitting. It should also handle bullet lists, numbered lists, semicolons, and conjunction-heavy sentences. Lists often contain compact facts that should not be merged into one large claim.

Citations such as [1], author-year references, or Evidence-2 markers should be removed before scoring because they can distort similarity. Pronouns should be kept when they are part of a sentence, but the surrounding context may be needed to interpret the subject.

Malformed answers should not crash the system. Empty strings, repeated punctuation, headings without content, and broken bullet markers should be ignored or converted into safe short claims.
