Climate Change

Climate change refers to long-term shifts in temperature, precipitation, sea level, and weather patterns. The current warming trend is mainly driven by increased greenhouse gases from human activities such as burning fossil fuels, deforestation, and some industrial processes.

Greenhouse gases such as carbon dioxide, methane, and nitrous oxide absorb infrared radiation and trap heat in the atmosphere. This process is natural, but human activity has increased greenhouse gas concentrations and strengthened the warming effect.

Observed impacts include rising global average temperatures, melting glaciers, shrinking Arctic sea ice, sea-level rise, ocean warming, and changes in extreme weather risks. Impacts vary by region and affect water resources, agriculture, ecosystems, infrastructure, and human health.

Climate responses include mitigation and adaptation. Mitigation reduces emissions or removes carbon dioxide from the atmosphere. Adaptation prepares communities for impacts that are already happening or expected. Clean energy, energy efficiency, resilient infrastructure, and ecosystem protection can all contribute.
