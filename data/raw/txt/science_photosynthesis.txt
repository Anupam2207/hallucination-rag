Photosynthesis

Photosynthesis is the process by which plants, algae, and some bacteria convert light energy into chemical energy. In plants, photosynthesis mainly occurs in chloroplasts. Chlorophyll absorbs light, and the plant uses that energy to convert carbon dioxide and water into glucose and oxygen.

The overall simplified equation for photosynthesis is carbon dioxide plus water, using light energy, produces glucose and oxygen. Glucose stores chemical energy that the plant can use for growth, repair, and reproduction. Oxygen is released as a by-product.

Photosynthesis has two major stages. Light-dependent reactions capture light energy and produce energy-carrying molecules. The Calvin cycle uses carbon dioxide to build sugars. Both stages are connected, but they perform different roles.

Photosynthesis is important for ecosystems because it produces much of the oxygen in the atmosphere and forms the base of many food webs. It also removes carbon dioxide from the air. However, photosynthesis rates depend on light intensity, carbon dioxide availability, temperature, water, and plant health.
