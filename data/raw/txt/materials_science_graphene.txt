Graphene

Graphene is a one-atom-thick sheet of carbon atoms arranged in a hexagonal lattice. It is the basic structural unit of graphite, but a single graphene layer has unusual electrical, mechanical, and thermal properties. Graphene is extremely strong for its thickness, conducts heat well, and can carry electrons with high mobility.

Researchers study graphene for potential uses in electronics, sensors, composite materials, energy storage, membranes, and flexible devices. Producing high-quality graphene at large scale remains challenging. Methods include mechanical exfoliation, chemical vapor deposition, and chemical reduction of graphene oxide. Graphene is not a universal replacement for silicon or metals; its usefulness depends on cost, manufacturability, device design, and integration with existing materials.
