Coral Reefs

Coral reefs are underwater ecosystems built by colonies of tiny animals called coral polyps. Reef-building corals live in a symbiotic relationship with algae known as zooxanthellae. The algae provide much of the coral's energy through photosynthesis, while the coral provides shelter and access to sunlight. Reefs support a high diversity of marine life, including fish, mollusks, crustaceans, and many other organisms.

Coral reefs protect coastlines by reducing wave energy and storm surge. They also support fisheries and tourism in many coastal regions. Coral bleaching occurs when corals lose their symbiotic algae because of stress such as unusually warm water. Bleached coral is not always dead, but prolonged bleaching can lead to high mortality. Pollution, overfishing, ocean warming, and ocean acidification are major threats to coral reef health.
