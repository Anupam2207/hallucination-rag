Second Language Acquisition

Second language acquisition is the process of learning a language after a first language has already been established. Learners develop vocabulary, grammar, pronunciation, listening comprehension, reading, writing, and pragmatic skills. Input, interaction, motivation, age, memory, identity, and learning environment all influence progress.

Comprehensible input helps learners understand messages slightly above their current level. Speaking practice and feedback help learners notice gaps and refine their language use. Adults can successfully learn new languages, although pronunciation and automatic fluency may be more difficult than for young children. Effective learning usually combines meaningful exposure, regular practice, spaced review, and opportunities to use the language for real communication.
