Federalism

Federalism is a system of government in which authority is divided between a central government and regional governments such as states or provinces. A federal constitution usually specifies which powers belong to the national government and which powers are reserved for regional units. Countries such as the United States, India, Germany, Canada, and Australia use federal systems.

Federalism can allow local governments to adapt policies to regional needs while preserving national unity. It can also create complexity when responsibilities overlap. Areas such as education, policing, taxation, healthcare, and environmental regulation may involve both national and regional authority. Federal systems require mechanisms for resolving disputes between levels of government, often through courts or intergovernmental negotiation.
