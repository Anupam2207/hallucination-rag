The Renaissance

The Renaissance was a period of cultural, artistic, scientific, and intellectual development in Europe that began in Italy in the fourteenth century and spread across the continent. Renaissance thinkers renewed interest in classical Greek and Roman texts while also developing new approaches to observation, perspective, anatomy, and human expression.

Artists such as Leonardo da Vinci, Michelangelo, Raphael, and Titian created works that emphasized realism, proportion, movement, and individual character. Linear perspective helped artists represent depth more convincingly. The printing press made books cheaper and helped ideas spread more widely. The Renaissance was not a sudden break with the Middle Ages; it was a complex period of continuity and change involving art, politics, religion, trade, and science.
