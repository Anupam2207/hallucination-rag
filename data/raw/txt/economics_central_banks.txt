Central Banks

A central bank is a public institution that manages a country's money supply, interest rates, financial stability, and sometimes banking supervision. Central banks often use monetary policy to influence inflation and employment. They can raise policy interest rates to slow inflationary pressure or lower rates to support borrowing and economic activity.

Central banks also act as lenders of last resort during financial stress. They may provide liquidity to banks when normal funding markets are disrupted. Many central banks target price stability, but their legal mandates vary across countries. Tools can include reserve requirements, open market operations, policy rates, asset purchases, and forward guidance. Central bank decisions affect exchange rates, credit conditions, asset prices, and household borrowing costs.
