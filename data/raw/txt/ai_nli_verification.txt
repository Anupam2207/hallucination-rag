Natural Language Inference for Claim Verification

Natural language inference, or NLI, compares a premise with a hypothesis. In claim verification, the evidence passage is the premise and the answer claim is the hypothesis. The model predicts entailment, contradiction, or neutral.

NLI can help when similarity alone is misleading. Two sentences can use similar words but disagree on a year, number, or entity. Evidence saying a model was introduced in 2020 contradicts a claim saying it was introduced in 2019.

NLI is computationally heavier than lexical rules or similarity scoring. On an 8 GB laptop, it should be optional and disabled in demo mode. Research mode can enable it for final evaluation if the model is available.

NLI results should be combined with answerability checks, retrieval confidence, and factual consistency rules. This makes the decision easier to explain and reduces dependence on a single model.
