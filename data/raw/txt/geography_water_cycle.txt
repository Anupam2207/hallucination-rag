Water Cycle

The water cycle describes the continuous movement of water through Earth's atmosphere, land, oceans, rivers, lakes, ice, and living organisms. It is powered mainly by solar energy and gravity.

Major processes in the water cycle include evaporation, transpiration, condensation, precipitation, infiltration, runoff, and groundwater flow. Evaporation changes liquid water into water vapor. Transpiration releases water vapor from plants. Condensation forms clouds when water vapor cools.

Precipitation returns water to the surface as rain, snow, sleet, or hail. Some water flows over land as runoff into rivers and oceans. Some infiltrates into soil and becomes groundwater. Water can also be stored in glaciers, lakes, wetlands, and aquifers.

The water cycle is important for weather, climate, agriculture, ecosystems, drinking water, and hydropower. Human activities such as urbanization, deforestation, groundwater pumping, and climate change can alter local and regional water cycles.
