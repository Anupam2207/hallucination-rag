Inflation

Inflation is a sustained increase in the general price level of goods and services in an economy. When inflation rises, the purchasing power of money falls because the same amount of money buys fewer goods and services than before.

Inflation can be measured using price indexes such as the consumer price index or producer price index. A price index tracks the cost of a basket of goods and services over time. Central banks and governments monitor inflation because it affects wages, savings, borrowing, investment, and living standards.

Causes of inflation can include demand-pull pressure, cost-push shocks, supply disruptions, currency depreciation, and expectations about future prices. Demand-pull inflation occurs when demand grows faster than supply. Cost-push inflation occurs when production costs rise and businesses pass those costs to consumers.

Moderate inflation can be manageable, but very high inflation creates uncertainty and reduces real incomes. Deflation, or a general fall in prices, can also be harmful if it discourages spending and investment.
